The gap

Interaction checkers stop at the drug pair.

Two medicines can be individually fine, mutually fine on paper, and still risky for this patient. Existing tools rarely account for who is taking them.

01

Same pill, different patient

A dosage that's safe for a 30-year-old with normal kidney function can be dangerous for a 70-year-old with reduced renal clearance — most checkers apply the same rule to both.

02

History is ignored

A patient who reacted badly to a similar compound before is flagged identically to someone with a clean history. Prior side effects are rarely fed back into the risk model.

03

No lifestyle context

Smoking and alcohol use materially change how drugs metabolize, yet they're almost never part of a standard interaction lookup.

What's new

Beyond pairwise interaction checks

01

Whole-regimen scoring, not pair-by-pair

PDCS treats the full medicine list plus the patient profile as one input, instead of checking each drug pair against a static table.

02

Personal history as a feature

Prior side effects and allergies shift the score for that specific patient, not just for the drug in general.

03

Built-in explainability

SHAP values expose exactly which factors pushed the score up or down, so the output is auditable rather than a black box.

04

Actionable, not just a warning

Alternative medicines and a safe dosage window are generated alongside the risk score, closing the loop for the patient.

Model stack

The algorithms behind the score

Baseline classifier

Random Forest

An ensemble of decision trees votes on a coarse risk tier from patient and drug features — robust to noisy or missing fields, which matters for real-world intake forms.

Refinement layer

XGBoost

Gradient boosting sharpens the Random Forest baseline, learning subtle non-linear combinations — like age × kidney disease × a specific drug class — that simple rule tables miss.

Explainability

SHAP

Shapley-value attribution breaks every prediction into per-feature contributions, so a clinician can see exactly why the score moved, not just what it is.
